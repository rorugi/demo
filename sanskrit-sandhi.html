Sandhi, step by step सन्धि

Join two words, or build a form from its root. Every step names the Pāṇini sūtra that applies and shows what changes; rules that don't fit are listed with the reason.

How the page works

Each pair goes through the same checks, in this order:

  1. Read the words. The input is split into sounds. IAST and Devanagari both work. Without diacritics, type long vowels doubled or as capitals (bhuu or bhU for bhū), H for ḥ, M for ṃ, sh and Sh for ś and ṣ, and T D N for ṭ ḍ ṇ. The line under each field shows how the word was read.
  2. End of word 1. A Sanskrit word may only end in a vowel or in k ṭ t p ṅ ṇ n m ḥ. Other final consonants are reduced first; final s and r become visarga. In a compound, a stem-final n drops (rājan → rāja-).
  3. Where the words meet. Depending on the last sound of word 1 and the first sound of word 2, the vowel, visarga or consonant rules are tried one after another. The first rule that fits is applied; the ones before it are listed as not applicable.
  4. Result. In IAST the words stay apart unless two vowels merge into one; in Devanagari a word ending in a consonant is written together with the next word, as in printed texts.

Covered: the external sandhi rules found in the standard tables (vowel sandhi, visarga, final m, n and stops, ch doubling). Not covered: internal sandhi inside a word (for example budh + ta → buddha), the change of n to ṇ after r ṛ ṣ, Vedic exceptions, and words with irregular behaviour beyond saḥ and eṣaḥ. A visarga typed as ḥ is treated as coming from s, except in common r-words such as punaḥ, antaḥ, prātaḥ; type the word with a final r to say it explicitly.

Sūtra numbers refer to Pāṇini's Aṣṭādhyāyī (book.chapter.rule).

Roots in the list

The derivation needs to know each root's class, whether it takes the connecting vowel iṭ, and its irregularities, so it works from a built-in list. Tap a root to load it.

How the derivation works
  1. Root and affix. The root gets the affix for the chosen form. Pāṇini's affixes carry marker letters (it) that are dropped but tell the grammar how to treat them: the k of kta means “no guṇa”, the p of tip means “guṇa allowed”.
  2. Present stem. In the present the root's class (gaṇa) decides the vikaraṇa between root and ending: a, ya, nu, u, nā, an infix na, reduplication or nothing. A few common roots have a special present stem (gam → gacch, sthā → tiṣṭha, dṛś → paśya).
  3. iṭ. Before the other affixes some roots insert i (seṭ roots), others don't (aniṭ).
  4. Changes inside the root: loss of a nasal, samprasāraṇa (vac → uc, yaj → ij) and guṇa (i → e, u → o, ṛ → ar).
  5. Where root and affix meet: the internal sandhi rules, among them Bartholomae's law (budh + ta → buddha), voicing and devoicing, ś → ṣ → k, the change of s to ṣ after i u ṛ k, and of n to ṇ after r ṛ ṣ.

The order of the steps follows the usual teaching order, not Pāṇini's exact rule ordering. Forms with prefixes (-ya absolutives), other persons and tenses, and roots outside the list are not covered.
