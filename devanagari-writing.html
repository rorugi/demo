Writing practice · broad nib

Devanagari writing for Hindi/Sanskrit क्ष

Only the centre lines, drawn with a flat nib at a fixed angle. Thick and thin come from the direction of the stroke alone.

Drag points to move them. Tap a point, then pick a tool below (hover for its name) or press Delete. Filled: points on the line; hollow: control handles. Two fingers: pinch to zoom, move together to scroll (same on a trackpad; with a mouse, Ctrl + wheel).

Strokes

    Splines

    The centre lines as SVG paths (cubic Bézier splines), in stroke order. Dots are drawn across the current nib angle. Coordinates are in font units: y points down, head line at y = 117, baseline at y = 700, width = adv. Variable width: the frame is as wide as the letter (adv + 40 on each side), good for setting words (next letter at x = adv). Fixed frame: 1200 × 1290 for every letter, centred, good for cards, icons and grids.

    
  
    

  
    
    
    How the nib works
    
    
      
      
    • Each stroke is a centre line stored as an SVG path. getPointAtLength() splits it into small steps.
      • 
      
    • Edit points: shows the points of the strokes of the current letter. Filled points lie on the line, hollow ones are the control handles of the curves. Drag to move a point; the bin removes the selected point. The point type sets which handles a point has: corner (no handles, drawn as a square), one handle (on one side only), two symmetric handles (in line and of equal length, so the stroke runs through smoothly) and two independent handles. “Smooth point” lays the handles of the selected point along the line from the previous to the next point, so the line passes through without a kink; “Smooth stroke” does this for every point of the stroke that lies between two curves, while corners and straight parts stay. The points themselves stay where they are. Changes are saved in this browser and also appear in the splines export; “Discard changes” restores the original strokes. Edited letters carry a small dot in the picker. If a built-in stroke is changed later, the old edit of that stroke no longer applies.
      • 
      
    • Nib: fixed is the broad nib with angle, width and thickness. Fitted to the font measures at each point of the centre line how wide the font is to the left and right, and sets the nib across the stroke to exactly that width. This gives the thick and thin of the font; angle and width then do not matter. Where strokes cross, outliers are limited, and at the end the real letter shape fades in.
      • 
      
    • Display: Canvas sets the nib down point by point on a <canvas>. SVG 1 only draws the centre line with stroke-dashoffset, at one width, without thick and thin. SVG 2 builds the whole nib shape as SVG and reveals it with a mask; for this the stroke is split into short pieces, so loops do not show too early. The SVG animations run in the browser itself (Web Animations API).
      • 
      
    • The step length is the distance between the points along the centre line where the nib is set down, in drawing units (the board is 1200 units wide) and converted to screen pixels. Long steps make the single nib positions visible.
      • 
      
    • The nib is a short edge at a fixed angle. Between two points the quadrilateral that this edge sweeps over is filled, on a <canvas>.
      • 
      
    • The grid follows the four zones of the script: at the top vowel signs such as i and e, in the middle two rows for the letter, at the bottom u, ū and ṛ. The head line lies on the border between the first and second row.
      • 
      
    • The font choice changes the letters in the picker and the outline for comparison. Sanskrit 2020 (© Omkarananda Ashram, SIL Open Font License) and Siddhanta Calcutta (© Mihail Bayaryn, Creative Commons BY-NC-ND 3.0, unmodified, non-commercial use) come with the page as files and load without installing. You can pick your own font files under “Load a font file …”; they are kept only in this browser. The strokes are the same for every font (drawn on Tiro Devanagari Hindi); the chosen font only shows behind them, and the comparison shows how much its shapes differ.
      • 
      
    • Where the stroke runs across the edge it gets wide; where it runs along the edge it gets narrow, but never thinner than the nib thickness. For this the quadrilaterals are also outlined with that thickness. There is no mask.
      • 
    
    
    
    The stroke order follows the common school variant, with the head line last.
