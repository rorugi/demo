How it works

In the text field you can write ITRANS or Devanagari; Devanagari, IAST and ITRANS are produced automatically. Wrap characters or words in ## to keep them unconverted, e.g. ##not##. Example: devanaagarii → देवनागरी → devanāgarī.

Check the result: some conversions are ambiguous. For example, an “a” inside a word is sometimes not pronounced in Hindi, but it is not removed automatically here.

Hindi and Sanskrit

Sanskrit mode uses a strict transliteration: aham → अहम् → aham; saMskRRitam → संस्कृतम् → saṃskṛtam. Final consonants get a halant ्, anusvāra ं becomes ṃ and visarga ः becomes ḥ. The simplified nasal notation is limited to Hindi and is not strict IAST.

“Drop final a” exists for Hindi only: it removes the last short “a” as a simple learning aid and is no substitute for a full pronunciation analysis.

Devanagari keys: क + ् + ष = क्ष; vowel signs follow the consonant, including ि. ITRANS is case-sensitive; IAST is an output format only. Designed for classical Sanskrit, without full support for all Vedic accent marks.

Keyboard

The keyboard shows which key carries each Devanagari character, which helps when learning to type. Open it in its own window with the window icon to keep it beside your word processor or browser: type or click into its small text box, then copy and paste. To type Hindi directly in every program, add the Hindi Traditional (InScript) layout in your operating system; the pop-out window then serves as a key map.
