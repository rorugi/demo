Syllable weight (guru and laghu)

Sanskrit metre counts every syllable as either light (laghu, ⏑, one mātrā) or heavy (guru, –, two mātrās). The orange marks the vowel of each heavy syllable.

  • Short vowels: a, i, u, ṛ, ḷ. Long vowels: ā, ī, ū, ṝ, ḹ, and e, ai, o, au, which are always long.
  • Heavy by nature: any syllable with a long vowel, e.g. nāyaṃ.
  • Heavy by position: a short vowel followed by
    • anusvāra ṃ or visarga ḥ: nāyaṃ, bhūyaḥ
    • two or more consonants (a conjunct, including kṣ and jñ): dharmakṣetre
    This also holds across a word boundary within a verse quarter: in ātmaiva hyātmano (Gītā 6.5) the final a of ātmaiva is heavy because h and y follow. The count restarts at every daṇḍa and line break. Positional heavies are shown with a dotted underline.
  • Everything else is light: a short vowel followed by at most one consonant, or by a vowel.

Weight belongs to the syllable, not the vowel: a short vowel before a conjunct is still pronounced short, but the syllable takes two mātrās in metre. Aspirates (kh, gh, th, bh …) are single consonants, and candrabindu (m̐) does not make a syllable heavy. Metres often treat the last syllable of a verse quarter as heavy regardless (anceps); that is not marked here. The popup also shows the word's weight pattern, e.g. – – ⏑.
